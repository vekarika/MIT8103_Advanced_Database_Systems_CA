MIT 8103 — Advanced Database Systems
Portfolio 1: Database Design and Modelling

Section 5: Data Requirements

5.1 Introduction

The CloudMart E-Commerce Management System requires a structured approach to managing the information generated by its online retail operations.

The database must store and maintain customer details, addresses, product information, categories, inventory records, orders, order items, payment records, and delivery information.

These data requirements establish the foundation for identifying database entities, attributes, relationships, and integrity constraints.

5.2 Customer Data Requirements

The database must maintain customer identifiers, names, email addresses, phone numbers, account creation dates, and account statuses.

Customer records must be uniquely identifiable, and the design should support appropriate validation of required customer information.

5.3 Address Data Requirements

The database must store customer addresses required for billing and order fulfilment.

Address records must contain sufficient information to identify the delivery destination. The design must establish how addresses relate to customers and how order records retain the address information applicable to a purchase.

5.4 Category Data Requirements

The database must maintain product categories to support product organisation and retrieval.

Category records must be identifiable and associated with the relevant products.

5.5 Product Data Requirements

The database must maintain product identifiers, names, descriptions, selling prices, category references, and product statuses.

The design must ensure that product information can be retrieved efficiently and that product records maintain valid category references where required.

5.6 Inventory Data Requirements

The database must maintain stock quantities and relevant inventory information for products.

Inventory updates must preserve consistency, and appropriate validation rules must prevent invalid stock quantities under the project's defined business rules.

5.7 Order Data Requirements

The database must maintain order identifiers, customer references, order dates, order statuses, and order totals.

Order records must be associated with the appropriate customers and support the retrieval of order histories.

5.8 Order Item Data Requirements

The database must record the products and quantities associated with individual orders.

Order-item records must identify the relevant order and product, record the quantity ordered, and preserve the applicable unit price at the time of purchase.

The design must support the representation of orders containing multiple products and products appearing in multiple orders.

5.9 Payment Data Requirements

The database must maintain payment identifiers, order references, amounts, payment methods, transaction references, statuses, and relevant dates.

Payment records must support reconciliation with orders and preserve the history of payment attempts or other payment events where required by the final business rules.

5.10 Delivery Data Requirements

The database must maintain delivery identifiers, order references, tracking information, delivery statuses, dispatch dates, and delivery dates.

The design must support retrieval of delivery information associated with an order and allow authorised updates to delivery status.

5.11 Data Integrity Requirements

The database design must support:

Unique identification of records.
Valid relationships between related records.
Validation of required information.
Appropriate restrictions on invalid numeric values.
Consistent representation of statuses.
Protection against invalid references.
Preservation of relevant historical transaction information.

These requirements will be translated into keys, constraints, and business rules during subsequent design stages.

5.12 Summary

The identified data requirements describe the information CloudMart needs to support its core business operations.

They will guide the identification of entities and attributes, the definition of relationships, and the development of the relational database schema.

The final model will be refined as the functional requirements and business rules are established.
