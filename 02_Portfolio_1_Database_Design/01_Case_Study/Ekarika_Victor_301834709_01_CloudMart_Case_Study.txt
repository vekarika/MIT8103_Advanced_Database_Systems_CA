2.2 Case Study Document

MIT 8103 — Advanced Database Systems
Continuous Assessment: Portfolio 1 — Database Design and Modelling

1. Case Study Title

CloudMart E-Commerce Management System

2. Organisation Overview

CloudMart is a simulated e-commerce business that provides an online platform for selling products to customers. The platform allows customers to browse available products, manage their accounts, place orders, make payments, and track deliveries.

The business requires a database system to organise and manage the information generated by its daily operations. The database will serve as the central data-management component of the platform, supporting product management, inventory tracking, order processing, payment recording, and delivery management.

CloudMart is a simulated organisation created for the MIT 8103 Advanced Database Systems Continuous Assessment.

3. Nature of the Business

CloudMart operates in the e-commerce and online retail sector.

Its principal business activities include:

Maintaining a catalogue of products.
Organising products into categories.
Managing customer accounts.
Monitoring product inventory.
Processing customer orders.
Recording payment transactions.
Coordinating product deliveries.
Generating operational and sales reports.

The business depends on accurate, consistent, and accessible data to support these activities.

4. Purpose of the Database System

The purpose of the CloudMart E-Commerce Management System database is to provide a structured and reliable way to store, retrieve, update, and manage information associated with the organisation's operations.

The database will be designed to:

Maintain customer and account information.
Store product details and product categories.
Track product inventory.
Record customer orders and their associated items.
Maintain payment records and transaction statuses.
Store delivery information and tracking statuses.
Support retrieval of customer order histories.
Support operational reporting and business analysis.
Maintain data integrity through appropriate database constraints.
Support reliable transaction processing and future scalability.
5. Intended Users

The proposed database will support the following user groups:

User group	Primary responsibility
Customers	Browse products, place orders, and view order histories
Sales and order staff	Review and manage customer orders
Inventory managers	Maintain product information and monitor stock
Finance and payment staff	Review payment records and transaction statuses
Delivery staff	Access delivery information and update delivery statuses
System administrators	Manage database access and system configuration
Management	Review sales, inventory, and operational reports

These are the intended users of the simulated system, not employees of an existing organisation.

6. Major Data Categories

The database is expected to manage the following categories of information:

Customer records.
Customer addresses.
Product records.
Product categories.
Inventory records.
Customer orders.
Order items.
Payment records.
Delivery records.

The specific attributes, data types, relationships, and constraints will be defined during the database design process.

7. Key Database Operations

The database will support the following operations:

Registering customers and maintaining customer information.
Adding and updating products.
Assigning products to categories.
Updating and monitoring inventory.
Retrieving product information.
Creating customer orders.
Recording the items associated with each order.
Recording payment transactions.
Updating order and payment statuses.
Managing delivery records and tracking statuses.
Retrieving customer order histories.
Generating sales and inventory reports.
8. Case Study Scope

The project will focus on the database requirements of CloudMart's core e-commerce operations.

The initial scope includes customer management, product management, inventory, order processing, payment records, and delivery tracking.

The project will examine how relational database design, SQL query optimisation, transaction management, NoSQL data modelling, and distributed or cloud database concepts can support the system.

Detailed implementation decisions will be made as the project progresses.

9. Case Study Assumptions

For the purposes of this assessment:

CloudMart is a simulated organisation.
Each customer is assigned a unique customer identifier.
Each product is assigned a unique product identifier.
Customer orders may contain multiple products.
A product may appear in multiple customer orders.
Orders, payments, and deliveries must be represented by identifiable records.
The system must preserve the relationships between customers, orders, products, and associated records.
The database must prevent invalid references between related records.
The design must support the practical activities required by the MIT 8103 CA.

These assumptions establish an initial design foundation. We will refine them when defining the business rules.
