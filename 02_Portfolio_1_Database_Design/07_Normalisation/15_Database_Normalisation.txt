CLOUDMART E-COMMERCE MANAGEMENT SYSTEM
DATABASE NORMALISATION

1. INTRODUCTION

Database normalisation is the process of organising data into related
tables to reduce unnecessary duplication and prevent insertion,
update, and deletion anomalies.

For the CloudMart E-Commerce Management System, normalisation helps
maintain consistent customer, product, order, payment, delivery, and
inventory information.

The design aims to satisfy First Normal Form (1NF), Second Normal
Form (2NF), and Third Normal Form (3NF).

2. FIRST NORMAL FORM (1NF)

Definition:

A relation is in First Normal Form when each attribute contains a
single atomic value for each record and there are no repeating groups
of attributes.

Application to CloudMart:

Customer contact details are stored in the Customer table.

Product information is stored in the Product table.

Each order item is stored as a separate record in Order_Item.

An order containing three different products has three Order_Item
records rather than storing all three products in a single field.

Similarly, multiple customer addresses are stored as separate
Address records linked to the customer.

Why this matters:

Storing multiple products or addresses in a single field would make
searching, updating, and validating individual values difficult.

The proposed design avoids these repeating groups by storing each
order item and each address as a separate record.

Conclusion:

The proposed CloudMart design supports 1NF by representing individual
values in separate attributes and using related records for repeating
business information.

3. SECOND NORMAL FORM (2NF)

Definition:

A relation is in Second Normal Form when it is in 1NF and every
non-key attribute depends on the whole of every candidate key.
This requirement is especially relevant to tables with composite keys.

Application to CloudMart:

The Order_Item table uses order_item_id as its primary key.

Its attributes include order_id, product_id, quantity, and unit_price.

The quantity and unit_price describe a particular product within a
particular order. They are not simply attributes of the Product table
because the same product can be purchased in different orders and
at different prices.

The Order_Item table separates order-line information from general
product information.

The Product table stores product attributes such as product_name,
description, selling_price, and category_id.

The Order table stores order-level attributes such as customer_id,
delivery_address_id, order_date, and order_status.

Why this matters:

Separating order-level, product-level, and order-line information
prevents product details from being unnecessarily repeated for every
order and prevents order details from being duplicated across all
items belonging to an order.

Conclusion:

The proposed structure supports 2NF by separating attributes according
to the entities and business facts they describe.

Because Order_Item uses a single-column primary key, a formal check
of 2NF must also consider its candidate keys and any additional
uniqueness rules.

4. THIRD NORMAL FORM (3NF)

Definition:

A relation is in Third Normal Form when it is in 2NF and non-key
attributes do not depend transitively on a key through another
non-key attribute.

Application to CloudMart:

Customer information is stored in Customer rather than being repeated
in every order record.

Product information is stored in Product rather than being repeated
in every Order_Item record.

Category information is stored in Category, while Product contains
category_id as a foreign key.

This means category details do not need to be repeated for every
product assigned to that category.

Address information is stored in Address, while Customer_Order stores
delivery_address_id to identify the selected delivery address.

Payment information is stored in Payment rather than being embedded
in the Customer_Order table. This supports multiple payment records
for one order.

Delivery information is stored in Delivery rather than being embedded
in the Customer_Order table. This supports multiple delivery records
for one order.

Why this matters:

These separations reduce duplication and help prevent update anomalies.
For example, changing a category description requires updating the
Category record rather than every product associated with that category.

Conclusion:

The proposed table structure is designed to support 3NF by separating
customer, address, category, product, order, payment, and delivery
facts into appropriate relations.

The final normal-form claim must be validated against the complete
functional dependencies and candidate keys of the implemented schema.

5. INSERTION, UPDATE, AND DELETION ANOMALIES

5.1 Insertion Anomaly

Problem:
If customer information and order information were stored in one large
table, adding a customer who had not yet placed an order could require
an incomplete order record or duplicated information.

Solution:
Customer information is stored separately from Customer_Order.

5.2 Update Anomaly

Problem:
If a product's description were repeated in every order record, changing
the description would require updating multiple records. Missing one
record could produce inconsistent information.

Solution:
Product descriptions are stored in Product and referenced by product_id.

5.3 Deletion Anomaly

Problem:
If customer details were stored only in an order table, deleting the
customer's last order could unintentionally remove the only record of
the customer's details.

Solution:
Customer records are stored separately from Customer_Order.

6. DERIVED VALUES AND NORMALISATION

CloudMart calculates the order-item subtotal as:

Line Subtotal = quantity * unit_price

The order total is calculated as the sum of the applicable order-item
subtotals under the initial business assumptions.

These values are treated as derived rather than independently
maintained source values to reduce the risk of inconsistent totals.

If a derived value is stored later for performance reasons, controlled
database logic must keep it consistent with the underlying records.

7. BENEFITS OF NORMALISATION TO CLOUDMART

The proposed normalised design provides the following benefits:

1. Reduces unnecessary duplication of customer and product information.
2. Improves consistency when customer, product, and category details
   change.
3. Supports referential integrity through primary and foreign keys.
4. Reduces insertion, update, and deletion anomalies.
5. Supports multiple products per order.
6. Supports multiple payment and delivery records per order.
7. Makes the schema easier to maintain and extend.

8. CONCLUSION

Normalisation provides a structured approach to organising CloudMart's
data.

The proposed design uses separate relations for customers, addresses,
categories, products, inventory, orders, order items, payments, and
deliveries.

The design is intended to support 1NF, 2NF, and 3NF. A rigorous
confirmation requires identifying the functional dependencies and
candidate keys for each relation and validating the final implemented
schema against them.
